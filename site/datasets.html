Teaching datasets

These are synthetic teaching fixtures, generated by python scripts/generate_data.py with NumPy seed 42. They contain no real people, properties, transactions, or rental records. They are supplied as course material; regeneration uses the installed numerical library versions, so byte identity across different versions is not guaranteed. The committed CSV files are the canonical classroom inputs.

Housing: 1,200 observations

Features are independently sampled: area uniformly between 35 and 220 square metres, bedrooms uniformly from 1–5, age uniformly from 0–70 years, distance uniformly from 0–30 kilometres, and three equally likely neighborhood categories. Price is in arbitrary currency units:

$$Y=50000+2400A+12000B-900G-1800D+N+\varepsilon,$$

where neighborhood premiums are 90,000 (central), 35,000 (residential), and 0 (outer), and independent noise is normal with standard deviation 25,000. Thirty-five ages are removed at random. This is an intentionally favorable linear model, not a realistic property valuation benchmark.

Churn: 1,800 observations

Tenure is 1–72 months; charges are uniform from 20–140; support calls are Poisson with mean 1.4. Contract probabilities are .55 monthly, .30 annual, .15 two-year. The generating log odds are

$$\log\frac{p}{1-p}=-1.8-.025T+.018(C-60)+.45S+K,$$

where contract effects are .9, −.4, and −1.1 respectively. Labels are independent Bernoulli draws conditional on features. Even the true probability model cannot perfectly classify these stochastic labels. These associations do not establish causal effects.

Bike demand: 2,000 observations

Independent hypothetical hourly scenarios; rows do not form a time series. Temperature, humidity, and wind are uniform on [0,35], [20,95], and [0,35]; hour, weekday, and season are uniform integers 0–23, 0–6, and 0–3. Season is an artificial index. The conditional Poisson mean is

$$\max\{5,35+5T-.12(T-23)^2-.45H-.8W+80e^{-((h-8)/2)^2}+110e^{-((h-17)/2.5)^2}+20\mathbf1_{d\ge5}+10s\}.$$

The nonlinear rush-hour terms motivate trees. Random splits are appropriate for these independent scenarios; real forecasts require chronological splits and features available at prediction time.

CSV values are rounded to four decimal places. Sources: authored for this repository, not downloaded datasets. Inspect scripts/generate_data.py for the precise generation order.

Additional datasets for lectures 04–07

  • Fraud and customer-segmentation data: rare Bernoulli outcomes and unlabeled customer geometry.
  • Support-ticket corpus: authored message families with template-group identifiers for honest splits.
  • Chronological daily sales: a time-indexed stochastic process for rolling one-step forecasts.

Run python scripts/generate_all_data.py from the repository root to regenerate all fixtures. The original generator remains available separately, and each extension generator uses an independent seed. The committed CSV files remain the canonical classroom inputs.
